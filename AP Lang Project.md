# **AP Language and Composition: Major Project & Essay**

## **The Rhetorical Atlas of Counter-Perspectives**

### **Part 1: Project Overview & Instructions**

#### **Project Objective**

In AP Language and Composition, we analyze how authors manipulate language, structure, and artistic style to persuade specific audiences. In this project, you will step into the role of the creator. You will explore a complex, multi-faceted debate by designing a multimodal, artistically rendered **Rhetorical Atlas**.

Rather than gathering outside sources or pitching a business product, you will **write three distinct original perspectives yourself**, create **custom visual artwork** to house those perspectives, and provide a **rigorous rhetorical self-analysis** of your choices.

#### **Core Topic Requirements**

Choose ONE abstract, complex controversy or enduring inquiry from the options below (or submit an original topic for teacher approval):

* *The Ethics of Algorithmic Governance vs. Human Discretion*  
*   
* *The Commodification of Memory and Personal History in the Digital Age*  
*   
* *The Tension Between Unchecked Technological Progress and Cultural Preservation*  
*   
* *The Definition and Ownership of Art in an Era of Synthetic Generation*  
* 

#### **Project Deliverables**

1. **The Artistic Container (Create)**

   * Design and construct a physical or digital visual framework that houses your three written pieces.  
   * *Format Options:* An illustrated illuminated manuscript/codex, a conceptual topographical map structure, or a three-panel interactive triptych.  
   * *Artistic Standard:* Your medium, color palette, texture, spatial layout, and visual motifs must intentionally mirror or contrast the rhetorical tones of the written perspectives.  
   *   
2. **Three Original Rhetorical Texts (Write)**  
     
   Write three original, highly stylized texts (500–700 words each) representing three distinct viewpoints on your chosen topic.  
   * **Perspective A (The Establishment / Traditionalist):** Written using classical Aristotelian structure, high diction, complex periodic sentences, and heavy reliance on deductive logic and ethos.  
   * **Perspective B (The Disruptor / Iconoclast):** Written in an urgent, provocative voice utilizing vivid imagery, non-traditional syntax, short emphatic sentences, and strong pathos.  
   * **Perspective C (The Synthesizer / Philosophical Observer):** Written in a measured, reflective tone utilizing qualified claims, subtle irony, nuance, and sophisticated stylistic devices.  
3. **Rhetorical Commentary & Marginalia (Analyze)**  
   * In the margins of your texts or in an accompanying analytical appendix, provide detailed rhetorical self-analysis for each piece.  
   * Identify and explain your specific syntactical choices, schemes, tropes, and tone shifts.  
   * Explain how your artistic choices (color theory, spatial orientation, visual symbols) reinforce the rhetorical strategies present in your written prose.

### **Part 2: Short Response Reflection Questions**

After completing your Rhetorical Atlas, respond to the following reflection questions in thoughtful, complete sentences (150–250 words per response).

1. **Voice and Style:** Which of the three rhetorical perspectives was the most challenging for you to inhabit as a writer? What specific syntactical or stylistic choices did you have to consciously force or modify to make that voice authentic?  
2. **Visual Rhetoric Integration:** How does your artistic container function as a argument in itself? In what ways does the visual medium convey meaning that text alone cannot?  
3. **Synthesis of Perspectives:** After adopting all three distinct positions, how did your personal perspective on the issue evolve? Did writing from a stance you personally disagree with alter your understanding of its rhetorical power?  
   

### **Part 3: AP Language Argumentative Essay Prompt**

**Time Limit:** 40 minutes (when written in class)

**Directions:** The following prompt requires you to draw upon your work with the Rhetorical Atlas as well as your broader knowledge of current events, history, literature, or personal experience.

#### **Prompt**

In his 1998 essay *Consilience: The Unity of Knowledge*, biologist E.O. Wilson argued that the greatest challenge facing modern society is not a lack of information, but our inability to integrate disparate, conflicting perspectives into a coherent understanding of truth. He noted that while specialization allows us to look deeply at parts of a problem, it often blinds us to the whole.

Carefully read the statement above. Then, in a well-written essay, develop a position on the extent to which engaging with opposing or radically different perspectives is necessary for achieving meaningful progress in contemporary society.

Use appropriate, specific evidence to illustrate and develop your argument.

#### **AP Essay Scoring Grid (6-Point Scale)**

* **Thesis (0–1 Point):**

  * *1 Point:* Responds to the prompt with a clear, defensible thesis that presents a nuanced position on the value of opposing perspectives.  
* **Evidence and Commentary (0–4 Points):**

  * *4 Points:* Provides specific, compelling evidence and line of reasoning that thoroughly explains how the evidence supports the thesis.  
  * *3 Points:* Provides specific evidence and clear commentary, though line of reasoning may have minor gaps.  
  * *2 Points:* Provides broad or limited evidence with repetitive or superficial commentary.  
  * *1 Point:* Provides simplistic or disconnected evidence with minimal explanation.

* **Sophistication (0–1 Point):**  
  * *1 Point:* Crafts a persuasive, complex argument by explaining nuances, identifying limitations of a proof, or demonstrating a consistently vivid and persuasive writing style.
