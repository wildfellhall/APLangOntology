# Passage-based MCQ construction

Use this reference when building or revising a question bank. Reviewed October 29, 2026 against the College Board CED sample items (printed pp. 113–120) and the current exam page. The following are original design inferences, not copied exam questions.

## What to preserve from the exam architecture

Reading items interpret nonfiction in context. Revision items operate on a supplied draft toward an explicit purpose. Current public samples use four choices and distinguish the two tasks. The current exam page describes five passage sets, with 23–25 reading and 20–22 writing questions. A ten-item single-skill set is a teaching arrangement, not a reproduction of that full-exam balance.

Reading prompts can ask about the work of a detail, an inference, the relationship between sections, a governing claim, or a limitation. Revision prompts can ask for an opening, conclusion, evidence, explanation, placement, deletion, transition, or sentence revision. Ask what a choice accomplishes in its situation. Do not equate spotting a term with explaining its rhetorical work.

## Author from evidence, not a stem template

1. Supply a complete, coherent stimulus before drafting questions. Reading passages need an identifiable speaker, situation, developed reasoning, and enough context to distinguish competing readings. Drafts need stable sentence numbers and plausible opportunities for revision. Label original simulations outside the text; never fabricate a real attribution.
2. Write the intended inference and its exact support. Record the paragraph or sentence anchors in data. An item should not become answerable merely by recognizing its skill label.
3. State one criterion in the stem. For revision, identify what the writer wants to accomplish. When two grammatically correct revisions differ rhetorically, the goal must decide between them. Evaluate each item independently against the original draft unless explicitly stated otherwise.
4. Write one best answer and three credible alternatives at the same conceptual level. Useful distractors misread scope, agency, function, source role, logical relation, or the requested revision goal. A true detail with the wrong function is often stronger than an invented event. Avoid comic irrelevance, hostile motive claims unsupported by the passage, and piles of absolute words that identify the key by moderation alone.
5. Explain each option with a passage-based reason. A rationale should identify the particular error, not say that an answer is irrelevant without explaining why. For an addition, distinguish information that is interesting from information that serves the specified purpose.

## Review the complete set

- Read every option as a possible key. If two fit, sharpen the purpose or revise the alternatives. Do not treat a preferred wording as uniquely correct without a stated reason.
- Verify sentence references after any stimulus edit. Check positions and pronoun antecedents in insertion items; the best insertion must work on both sides.
- Audit lexical clues: a copied keyword, unusually careful qualification, conspicuously longer key, or three implausible distractors can bypass comprehension. Length statistics identify candidates for review; they cannot assess item quality.
- Keep option order stable once responses are saved. Rotate or permute choices deterministically before publication; version materially changed items so an old answer is not silently reinterpreted.
- Count unique questions separately from assignments. If concepts share a skill set, say so and preserve one response per item across those pages. A quantity requirement must be checked against actual IDs and skill mappings, not repeated display instances.
- When the requirement is ten exclusive items per concept, assign every canonical ID to one concept and author the missing items. Preserve original questions as alternate skill-review views without counting them twice. Check concept ownership, unique stems, figure availability, sentence references, and complete explanations in both the rendered page and downloaded assignment.
- Match coverage to the requested unit of practice. Distinguish a narrow editorial concept from its broader official CED skill. A shared 7.A set does not imply ten separate questions specifically about every named device.
- Use learner attempts and expert review to refine ambiguity and difficulty. A passing schema test does not validate AP equivalence, discrimination, or instructional effectiveness.
