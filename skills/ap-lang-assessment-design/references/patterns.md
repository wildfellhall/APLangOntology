# AP Language patterns and evidence

Reviewed October 28, 2026. These are design inferences from the sources below, not quotations from official materials.

## Reading and writing items

The current CED sample distinguishes passage interpretation from purposeful revision and presents four answer choices. Read the full context before drafting an item. A useful reading stem identifies a paragraph, phrase, contrast, or example and asks for its function. A useful revision stem specifies the writer's purpose, then asks which addition, deletion, arrangement, or replacement best achieves it.

Original example: a volunteer concedes that evening library access costs money, then proposes a term-long trial. A reading item can ask how the concession prepares the proposal. A revision item can ask what addition would help cost-conscious officials evaluate the trial. A staffing estimate fits; a useful but unrelated history of the building does not. For advanced practice, make wrong options plausible competing interpretations, not obvious nonsense.

Audit the key against the whole passage. Ask whether two answers could satisfy the stated goal. If so, sharpen the criterion or change an option. Explain a distractor's specific error rather than saying only that it is wrong. Do not create line-number questions without stable line numbering; paragraph references work better in responsive websites.

## Essays

Synthesis: an issue with competing considerations, a usable six-source packet, a position-taking task, at least three identified sources, and reasoning that places sources in conversation. A data source needs units, denominators, sampling context, and limits. A visual source needs an actual visual and accessible description. Preserve all source qualifications.

Rhetorical analysis: contextual framing + full passage + interpretive task. Evidence is textual detail; commentary explains the work of the choice within the writer's developing argument. Avoid “identify three devices” as the essay's controlling task.

Argument: introduce a contestable issue and ask for a position. Permit evidence from knowledge and experience while requiring accurate specifics. Do not force students to support the view embedded in the introduction. A qualification is valuable when its condition changes what the claim means.

The current official exam page lists 45 MCQs in 60 minutes and three essays in 135 minutes; weights are 45% and 55%. Use the current page for future updates. Suggested practice pacing can offer 40 minutes for analysis/argument and 15 minutes reading plus 40 writing for synthesis, while allowing untimed practice.

## Integrated project pattern

Begin with an inquiry whose competing values cannot be resolved by a slogan. Have students compose distinct positions for defined audiences; design a visual form that represents a relationship; annotate textual and visual choices; write an evidence-based position that survives an objection; revise after feedback; and reflect with before/after examples and a transfer question.

The supplied Rhetorical Atlas example inspired this integration. Adaptations made for the library: no unverified attributed Wilson claim, no equation of project percentages with AP scores, no fixed traditionalist/high-diction versus disruptor/short-sentence stereotypes, and explicit source/invention distinctions. The original artifact remains unchanged.
